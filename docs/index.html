Your Mac has a touch screen.
macOS pretends it doesn't.

Plug a touch monitor into a Mac and you can tap to click — and that's it. Touch Enabled Mac fills in the rest: natural scrolling, an iPad-style keyboard that appears when you need it, and controls big enough for fingers.

The problem

Touch monitors work beautifully with Windows and iPads. Connect one to a Mac and you hit three walls immediately:

🚫

Scrolling doesn't work

macOS only tracks a single finger, so the two-finger swipe you use everywhere else does nothing. Every scroll means finding a scrollbar.

⌨️

No on-screen keyboard

Tap a text field and… nothing. You reach back for the physical keyboard, defeating the point of a touch setup.

🎯

Targets made for a mouse

Video controls, seek bars and menus are built for a precise pointer — frustrating and error-prone with a fingertip.

The fix — two small tools

A native menu-bar app for the system, and a Chrome extension for the browser. Each works alone; together they make touch feel first-class everywhere.

macOS app

Touch that works in every app

  • Two-finger swipe scrolling — natural-direction, horizontal and vertical, on any touch monitor.
  • Keyboard on focus — the iPad-style keyboard slides in when you focus any input box, in any application.
  • One menu-bar switch — auto-detects touch screens, with a per-monitor toggle and a ⌥⌘K shortcut.

Chrome extension

Touch-friendly browsing

  • Touch media controls for YouTube — big play/pause, prev/next, ±10 s, volume and seek targets.
  • A keyboard on every site — works with any input field, including React/Vue apps and strict sites like Google.
  • Live predictions — word completions and YouTube search suggestions as you type.
